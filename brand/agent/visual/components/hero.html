hero

The display band. Dark bed, one headline, two calls to action — and the only place in the entire system where lime type is legal.

Use it when

  • Opening a page. Once, at the top.
  • You need one big statement in lime — this is the surface that makes 12.41:1 possible.
  • You have already read ../artifacts/web/page-composition.md and know the other eight sections are coming.

Don't use it when

  • It would be the whole page. A hero is not a landing page.
  • You want a headline sitting directly on the photograph. Forbidden by name.
  • There is already a hero above it.

Default — no photograph

The preferred hero. The coast belongs in a bounded frame further down the page, not behind the headline.

The headline is fixed

One line, and it is not a matter of taste: Compile once, output anywhere. It is on the concept art, it is the brand's whole promise, and ../../verbal/messaging.md states it as production copy rather than as guidance about copy.

Never count the targets. Not "Ship six", not "One source. Six outputs.", not "six frameworks". Counting makes the promise sound conditional and small — as if the answer to "does it work with mine?" were a list you have to check. Name individual frameworks where a specific reader needs to find their own; never as a total.

The rules this component enforces

  • Headline is --step-7, Black 900, lime, on the dark bed. One per page.
  • Subhead is never lime — body-weight copy is never lime on any surface.
  • If the photograph appears, the scrim is mandatory and is a real element in the markup.
  • Decorative stickers sit in the margins and disappear below 60rem, because scattered absolute placement collapses at 390px.
  • The wordmark, the buttons and the decorative stickers all carry filter: var(--elev-*). A flat hero is the failure this rebuild exists to correct.

card

A panel of paper on a sheet of paper. Longer than a label, shorter than a section — and the preferred home for the photograph.

Use it when

  • Grouping content that is longer than a label and shorter than a section.
  • An audience segment, a feature, an objection and its answer.
  • You need the coast image on the page — bounded, die-cut, with no text on it.

Don't use it when

  • It is really a page section. Sections are beds, not panels.
  • It holds one line — use callout or a badge.
  • You are about to render nine identical ones. That is what a page looks like when nobody decided what mattered.

Plain

Differentiator

Activation-neutrality

React, Vue, Svelte, Solid and Angular hydrate. Qwik resumes. These are architecturally different ways to bring a page to life, and porting between them by hand is a rewrite.

Proof

The oracle

An equivalence check that can't fail proves nothing. Ours is mutation-tested: we deliberately break the compiler and confirm the tests catch it.

Exit plan

Ejectable by design

There's no Frameless runtime in your bundle. Delete the compiler tomorrow and the code keeps working. That's the entire exit plan, and it's deliberate.

Colour

A card is allowed to be loud. react.gg's page is built out of saturated blocks with thick cream borders and hard offset shadows, and that is the register to reach for when a section needs to carry weight rather than carry text. Set --card-fill and --card-ink; the eyebrow and footer retune with them.

Photo — the preferred way to use the coast

A bounded die-cut image with no text on it. This is Tier 2, and it is more on-brand than any full-bleed treatment: the coast is a photo sticker. The caption sits outside the cut, on the cream, where its contrast is a property of a known colour rather than of a photograph.

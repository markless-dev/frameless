framework-badge

Die-cut stickers, one per compilation target, in that framework's own colour, with its real logo in full colour and its name in white.

Use it when

  • Naming the targets: a targets section, a nav, a table header, the footer.
  • You want breadth to be checkable — a reader should find their own framework on the page.
  • In canonical order: React, Vue, Svelte, Solid, Angular, Qwik.

Don't use it when

  • You need decoration. These are trademarks used nominatively, not brand texture.
  • You were going to count them. Never total the targets — see ../../verbal/messaging.md.
  • You were going to colour a mark in --accent. Never.

The set

Full colour, glossy, elevated, tilted. Each band is tinted to its own hue and each keyline is the dark end of that hue — a neutral black keyline on a saturated fill is what made the previous version read as printed-out rather than die-cut.

Large — carrying the activation model

React, Vue, Svelte, Solid and Angular hydrate. Qwik resumes. Two architecturally different ways to bring a page to life, from one source — the strongest technical hook the brand has, and this is where a sticker can carry it.

Small — for a dense row

The mark and the name on one line. Everything else is unchanged: still a fill, still a tinted keyline, still a band, still a shadow. A sticker does not stop being a sticker because it got smaller.

The grey silhouette

What a sticker collapses to when one of its siblings is selected — the state framework-carousel is built on. It loses colour entirely. A dimmed sticker reads as disabled; a desaturated one reads as simply not the one you are looking at, and it is what makes the selected sticker read as lit.

Interactive

Hover or tab through these. The object rises a couple of pixels and its shadow grows a step — the growing shadow is the part the eye actually reads as lift.

The measured colours

Sampled from the concept art in Chromium, by drawing it to a canvas and reading pixels. Two statistics per badge: the block average, which is what a naive sample gives, and the mean of the top 5% most saturated pixels, which is what the fill actually is. The kit is anchored on the second. Anchoring on the first produced the version the owner rejected.

Colour: what is permitted, and the one thing that is not

Mark colourAllowedNotes
The project's own brand coloursYes — the defaultWhat the concept art shows and what this component ships.
WhiteYesWhere the project's own pairing is a white mark on its colour — Svelte and Angular.
The grey silhouetteYesThe deselected carousel state, and only that.
--accentNeverTrademark risk and brand confusion. A lime React logo reads as a Frameless product rather than as a reference to React.
